# CDB JobTread Estimating

## When to use

Use to develop or revise scope, quantities and pricing before a JobTread budget/proposal. Load [jobtread](../jobtread/SKILL.md) for all integration work and [budget writing](../jobtread-budget-writing/SKILL.md) for commercial terminology and customer presentation. A wording-only request is not permission to re-estimate. No rate table, customer record or historical project example is shipped here.

## Procedure

1. **Gather inputs.** Identify the exact job/package, latest plans and revision, specifications, customer request, site observations, actual supplier/trade quotes, existing budget and accepted documents. Preserve originals. Record source/page/revision and distinguish current from superseded evidence. Start a new estimate with a brief explanation: inputs → resolve scope → approve estimating baseline → price scope → structure proposal. On resumption, identify current stage rather than repeating intake.
2. **Resolve scope before pricing.** Read available sources before questioning the user. Ask grouped, dependency-aware questions about work boundaries, responsibilities, inclusions/exclusions and material unknowns. Distinguish a drawing requirement, user decision, assumption and unresolved fact. Recommendations and partial answers are not approved decisions. Resolve contradictions openly; do not silently discard code/plan requirements or infer new work from generic notes.
3. **Maintain one working estimate per package in the approved private job location.** Use three sections: Scope (agreed work, sources/decisions, exclusions and remaining technical inputs), Pricing (same scope item identifiers, calculations and basis), and Proposal structuring (allocations into customer items). Do not establish new permanent Dropbox folders or duplicate scope/proposal trackers. Discover an existing authoritative estimate first.
4. **Lock the estimating baseline only with user approval.** Record revision, date and approver as 'Locked for estimating'. This identifies what is being estimated, not customer acceptance or permission to issue. Material unresolved quantities or methods require confirmation or an expressly approved estimating basis. If work/exclusions/assumptions change, record the decision, increment and re-lock the revision before affected repricing; technical confirmation without scope change needs no invented revision.
5. **Estimate each scope category once.** Reference the locked revision and same scope IDs. Establish measured quantities/units, installation method, labor/crew hours, loaded rates, supplied materials, equipment/handling/disposal, protection, oversight and applicable support costs. Assign overlapping work to one owner; distinguish supply from installation, rough from finish visits, and production hours from curing/inspection lead time. Missing inputs are unknown, not zero. Do not infer measurements without a verified scale or takeoff basis.
6. **Make pricing traceable.** For each basis identify actual quote, approved historical comparison, sourced current market research or explicit user estimate; date and qualify it. Keep unverified judgment visible, never fabricate a quote or market rate. Resolve material pricing variables rather than silently picking finishes, tiers or methods. Confirm project-specific markup, margin, tax, contingency and responsibility; use decimal arithmetic, distinguish markup from margin, and follow the writing skill's rounding rules. Do not import old private rate tables, fixed percentages or unsupported standard allowances.
7. **Structure the customer proposal after scope pricing.** Combine/split scope prices into clear customer items without changing work. Map every allocation back to its scope ID, reconcile allocation sums against each scope price and reconcile total once; never add a full scope price again after splitting it. Keep internal cost calculations, source interviews and unresolved questions out of the signable presentation. Preserve necessary agreed exclusions and qualifications using the writing skill, not defensive boilerplate.
8. **Apply only requested JobTread changes.** Use [budget intake](../jobtread/references/budget-data-intake.md) for exact source-to-budget mapping, existing record IDs, budget/document separation and readback. Preparing an estimate does not authorize replacing the live budget, changing an accepted baseline, deleting items or sending a proposal. Use [customer documents](../jobtread/references/customer-documents.md) for separately requested draft/issue actions. Preserve issued versions and handle later accepted-scope changes through the approved change process.

## Pitfalls

- Customer target budget is context, not established cost or an approved selling price.
- 'Labor only' does not decide equipment, rough materials, disposal or owner-supplied installation responsibilities.
- Do not price unapproved missing scope, invent trade terminology, copy legacy cost rules, or treat an estimating lock as contract acceptance.
- Scope descriptions and presentation are owned by budget writing; do not maintain conflicting allowance or rounding rules here.

## Verification and handoff

Reconcile every scope item to a priced item or explicit unresolved/excluded decision. Verify quantities, units, arithmetic, scope overlap, cost/price/tax basis, allocations, approved revision and source currency. Read exact budget targets/totals back after authorized writes; confirm unrelated fields and issued documents stayed unchanged. Report estimate maturity, basis, approved totals/deltas, missing inputs and whether any document was actually issued. An incomplete scope or unexplained price is a draft, not a finished estimate.
